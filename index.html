Encryption is partial

Asset bundles are not encryptedconfirmed

Every .uab opens with a plain UnityFS signature, format 8. YooAsset's optional encryption was left off, so models, textures and artwork are readable without any key.

The client config is encryptedconfirmed

ClientConfig.json uses a 16-byte container header whose field at offset 12 holds the payload length. The 2,160-byte payload is a multiple of 16, measures 7.92 bits of entropy per byte across all 256 values, repeats no block, and shows no repeating XOR key at lengths 1 through 16. That points to a block cipher, most likely AES in CBC or CTR mode.

72 bf 3d 6e | a9 07 12 0c | 00 00 00 00 | 70 08 00 00
     ?              ?             = 0           = 2160

Species

Creatures are called Parmon internally. IDs are five digits: the first four name the family line, the fifth marks a position within it. Two extra digits flag a variant.

97 species playable at launchconfirmed

Counted from the skeletons in Res_Export/Character/Avatar. It matches the figure community databases report, while being derived independently of them.

63 family lines, from 1 to 7 membersconfirmed

Eleven lines hold three members, twelve hold two, eight have no evolution at all. Just one has seven — 1002x — which makes it either an unusually large family or a thematic grouping rather than an evolution chain.

Production stops sharply after ID 1054likely

Rigged-model coverage falls from 80% to 15% past that mark. Since IDs are assigned as production proceeds, the cliff looks like a reserve of unfinished content.

The compendium references 139 speciesconfirmed

308 UI_Img_ResearchBook_Enter_<id> images cover 139 distinct IDs against 97 playable ones, so the in-game journal is built for more entries than launch fills.

A few species have male and female formsconfirmed

Species 10161 and 10162 carry separate _Male and _Female artwork — eleven images for 10161 alone. No other species does. Casing drifts between files (10161female, 10161_Male), which suggests conventions were never harmonised between artists.

Names

The English text table holds 112,065 strings. Creature names were isolated by a marker test: only creatures are ever prefixed with a form word (Alpha, Umbral, Prismana, Omega) or suffixed with Family, Relic or Egg. No interface label collects those.

71 creatures namedconfirmed

Each name below carries at least two independent markers, which rules out interface strings. The chips show which markers were found.

Named family lines

Twenty-eight lines appear in the text as "<name> Family", which is the game's own grouping of an evolution chain.

Evolution chains, reconstructed from name stemslikely

Shared prefixes line up with the four-digit line prefixes found in the asset IDs, so these chains are probable but not proven. The order within each chain is inferred from name length and evolution conventions.

Which name belongs to which IDunresolved

Text entries are addressed by 32-bit hashes, and no hash function tested (CRC32, FNV-1, FNV-1a, djb2, sdbm, masked and unmasked) matched more than a handful of the 66,882 known identifiers. The one file that holds both the name keys and the creature data, pmdata.bin, turns out to be a serialised Lua heap dump: 32-bit pointers and inline doubles, not record arrays. Mapping names to IDs would mean rebuilding the heap layout, which is out of proportion to the two days left before the names are simply readable in the game.

The roster, named and numbered

Every creature in the game carries a compendium number alongside its display name, and both sit in the same record inside the game's design-data blob. Recovering one gives the other.

93 creatures mapped to their asset IDconfirmed

The blob stores 109,087 text keys in its record region, against 705 expected by chance. Names cluster with a stable numeric field that repeats identically across every occurrence of a creature, hundreds of kilobytes apart. That field is the compendium number, and the asset ID follows from it directly.

The mapping validates against three independent sourcesconfirmed

Besauce at 10122, Blazen at 10222, Infergon at 10025 and Helgon at 10023 were each recovered earlier from animation clip and boss script filenames, by a method that shares nothing with this one. All four agree. The Iris line — 10211, 10212, 10213 — had been predicted from name length alone and lands exactly.

Family codenames agree too, across all 34 lines: the Crab line holds Budclaw, Shrubclaw and Geoclaw; ShellStar holds Shelly, Sheldon and Sherro; DungBeetle holds Baleetle and Waleetle; SleepyBear holds Cubbo and Grizbo. Thirty-four lines, no mismatch.

Two earlier claims correctedrevised

10022 and 10024 were previously attributed to Helgon because his animation clips appear in their asset lists. That was a shared asset, not a name. They are Helmwhelp and Jawling. The record data is the stronger source and supersedes the filename inference.

Internal codenames

Artists name their animation clips after what the creature actually is. Those names survive in the asset paths, which makes them readable without touching a single encrypted file.

Ten IDs matched to a display nameconfirmed

Each of these appears written out in an animation clip or a boss script filename, and each matches a name independently confirmed in the text table. Two examples: ST_Monster_AutoCombat_Boss_10222_BLAZEN.lua and OMap_Parmon_10025_Infergon.

32 family lines carry a codenameconfirmed

Every clip in a line shares one descriptor, which identifies what the family is even where the commercial name is unknown. 87 of the 97 playable IDs are covered.

The Iris line resolves completelylikely

The text table holds Iris, Irisal and Irisalis; line 1021x has exactly three members; and all three carry the Iris codename. The order follows name length, which makes 10211 Iris, 10212 Irisal and 10213 Irisalis. The 1002x line works the same way, with Helm on 10021, 10026 and 10027 and Helgon on 10022 through 10025, against Helmut, Helmwhelp, Helgon, Helion and Hellsoul in the text.

Codenames are descriptors, not display namescaveat

Otter, Crab, Seagull and Godzilla are what the art team called these creatures internally. They say what each family is, not what players will see on screen. Only the ten pairs above are actual display names.

Systems

The design-data index names 972 tables in plain text. Table names are written by the people who build the features, so the index doubles as a table of contents for the whole game.

Twenty systems, 730 of the 972 tablesconfirmed

Grouped by name prefix below. The count beside each system is how many tables it owns, which is a fair proxy for the machinery behind it. Creature handling leads with 136; the homeland and its camping car take 101, more than the roguelike, PvP, Boss Rush and Egg Snatch combined. The 242 names left over are engine plumbing — triggers, scenes, resource maps, input bindings.

What the names tell us

Egg Snatch is a full mode, not a side activityconfirmed

Thirty-three tables cover it, including chips and chip slots, equipment, weapon durability with a consume rate, a repair kit, a talent tree, a calcination system for collections, and a daily-refreshing ranked ladder with its own reward boxes. The Lua scripts add a team lobby for it.

The roguelike runs on dice and a bookconfirmed

Thirty-seven tables: dice, room affixes, random events with branching options, a multi-level talent tree, difficulty tiers, seasons, a weekly boss with its own rewards, and a "rogue book" holding bosses, buffs and events. A separate transform table suggests creatures change during a run.

Homeland is the largest non-creature systemconfirmed

A hundred and one tables covering plots, buildings, blueprints, furniture placement, a crafting formula system with random outcomes and periods, a material shop, a lottery, trash, orders, and a seasonal layer with celebrations, collections and mutations. The camping car alone has ten tables for components, upgrades, plates and interior models.

Fishing capture is its own minigameconfirmed

Ten tables with balls and ball values, battle grades, a petal source, progress and reward-progress tracks, and a time table. The debug strings mention tickets, floors, energy and catches, and a CRIT distribution — so it is scored, not just flavour.

Catch rate is computed, not fixedconfirmed

Five tables feed the catch probability: the creature's AI state, its AI tags, its entity tags, and any buffs on it each carry their own modifier table. So approaching a creature unnoticed, or applying a status first, changes the odds. A separate absorb table and a lucky-reward display round it out.

Boss Rush and Catch Rogue are separate modesconfirmed

Boss Rush has fourteen tables: buffs, cycles, grades, levels, seasons and their rewards, plus its own UI tags. Catch Rogue has seven more — buffs, phases, levels with finish conditions, NPCs and rewards — and sits apart from the main roguelike tables. Two distinct run-based modes, not one.

A chemistry layer in the worldlikely

Chemistry element tables sit beside material reaction, voxel reaction and tag reaction tables, with a chemical material template exported from the editor. Read together, they point at elemental interactions playing out on world objects rather than only in combat — wet grass conducting electricity, that kind of thing. The names support the shape; nothing seen so far confirms the specifics.

Smaller systems worth naminglikely

A piano with music, selection and timbre tables. Quizzes in single and multiple-choice form with a question bank. A photo studio with lighting, filters, ornaments, DIY presets and a "wonderful replay" capture. NPC duels tied to map marks. Vehicles with seats and seat skills. A world racing track. A tree-climbing table. A badge system in five tables. And a decibel grade table, which has no obvious counterpart in anything seen so far.

From the interface

Catching has ten distinct typesconfirmed

Normal, Boss, Behind, Group, Leader, Once, Flash, Rainbow, ShiningStar and LevelA, each with its own icon. Boss catching uses a separate ball asset from the standard one.

Gear rarities and chipsconfirmed

Five rarities — white, green, blue, purple, gold — and two chip families, arm and weapon, in five categories each.

Named charactersconfirmed

Bohr, Sorora, Fernanda, Ling, Nicole, Levi, Oswen, Sunia, Anthi, Sisus and Elder Jaeger all have dedicated animation sets. Three creatures carry a character name in their asset path: 10194 with Lita, 10213 with Irelia, 10233 with Velouria, 10221 with Frizz and 10323 with Knight.

Open questions

What is still unresolved, most interesting first.

The 202xx seriesto verify

Two compendium images carry IDs 2021102 and 2021203. That series appears nowhere else — not in the Parmon list, not as a skeleton, not as a texture. An entire family absent from the rest of the client, and the strangest thing in this survey.

Species 10193 is a quieter version of the same oddity: it sits between 10192 and 10194, has journal artwork, and yet never appears under a Parmon_ path. Three IDs in total are known only through the compendium, which is why the census counts 182 rather than 179.

Display namesto verify

Everything is indexed by numeric ID. The lookup table to commercial names sits in the localisation bundles, which are reachable since nothing there is encrypted. This is the step that makes the whole survey readable to an audience.

The lua folderto verify

14 .bin files totalling 200 MB. If that is decompilable Lua bytecode it holds gameplay logic — damage formulas, evolution conditions, encounter tables. Not yet inspected.

Empty partitionsto verify

Partition files water_26 and fire_27 weigh 137 bytes against 858 KB for ark_25. They are numbered in sequence but hold nothing. Four test1–test4 partitions are in the same state.

The 41 IDs with no assetsto verify

Nothing separates upcoming content from cut content here. Low IDs (10082, 10101, 10113) are more likely abandoned; high ones (10631 through 10783) more likely held in reserve.

How to phrase this publicly

The defensible wording stays descriptive: the compendium references 139 species while 97 are playable at launch, and 12 of the rest already carry partial 3D assets. Announcing "60 new creatures confirmed" does not hold — this survey cannot separate what is coming from what was dropped.

Method

Everything here comes from reading files offline, game closed, on a copy of the install folder. No debugger, no injection, no memory access.

Primary source

The content manifest — 25.7 MB of plain text, one asset path per line:

Aniimo_Data/cvs/res/patchv2/res/ver/3509129/win/
  PackageManifest_assetxbt_DefaultPackage_3509129_stab.txt

The ver/3509129 segment is versioned per build. A new folder appears with every patch, which makes it possible to diff manifests and isolate exactly what an update adds.

Artwork extraction

PAT = re.compile(r'ResearchBook_Enter', re.I)

Walk all 2,075 bundles with UnityPy, keep Texture2D and Sprite objects whose name matches, write them out as PNG.

Text and script archives

Game scripts ship in LuaScripts.xdf, a plain ZIP holding 8,608 uncompressed Lua files, indexed by a readable JSON manifest in LuaScripts.xdt. Compiled scripts are LuaJIT 2.0 bytecode (magic 1B 4C 4A 02) and still carry the studio's source paths in their headers.

Localisation is split in two: NewTextMap_<lang>.json maps a hash to an [offset, length] pair, and Compress_<lang>.bin holds the UTF-8 bytes those pairs point into. Fifteen languages ship, English included.

off, ln = index[key]
text    = blob[off:off+ln].decode("utf-8")

Diffing the launch patch

The content manifest lives under ver/<build>/, so the launch build will land beside 3509129 rather than replacing it. Keeping a copy of the current manifest makes the first diff a one-line comparison, and that diff is the cleanest way to see exactly what day-one adds.
